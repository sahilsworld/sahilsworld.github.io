QUESTIONS I KEEP COMING BACK TO

Interests.

Biology, computation, and what we can build with both.

I’m interested in how we can use computing to understand and engineer biology—and what biology can teach us about computing. These are some of the directions I want to explore.

AI that learns from experiments

I’m excited by the idea of AI systems that propose an experiment, help carry it out, and use the results to decide what to try next. Autonomous labs bring together a lot of what interests me: software, robotics, biology, and learning from the physical world.

Digital twins & molecular design

How much can we learn about a biological system before testing an idea in the lab? I’m interested in models of cells and patients, simulations of laboratory experiments, and AI tools for designing molecules. I want to understand how predictions become useful experiments—and where the models fall short.

Biology as a computer

The other direction fascinates me too: using living systems to compute. Neurons that learn, biological circuits, and interfaces between cells and electronics make me curious about what computing could look like beyond conventional hardware.

From an idea to something useful

I’m drawn to biotech startups and the work of turning research into tools people can use. I’m especially curious about what small teams can build by combining AI, experimental partners, and a good scientific question.

Beyond the lab

  • Philosophy & a fulfilling life. How do we decide what matters, balance ambition with enjoying life, and make room for the people around us?
  • Community & leadership. I like bringing people together and thinking about what makes a group feel like a community.
  • Learning & expression. Music, picking up new skills, and exploring unfamiliar places give me different ways to learn and express myself.
